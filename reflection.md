# Mess2Meal — my project reflection

> **Draft for my review:** I have written this in first person based on the work documented in this repository and the research I reviewed. I must check that every sentence reflects my own experience before submitting it. I have not claimed that I completed fieldwork.

When I started Mess2Meal, I focused mainly on building a system that could list surplus food and match it with possible recipients. During the research, I realised that food redistribution is not just a matching problem. Timing, food handling, shelf life, recipient capacity and a confirmed handover are equally important. I also learned that India already has initiatives such as IFSA and volunteer networks such as Robin Hood Army. Because of this, I should position Mess2Meal as a small coordination prototype rather than claiming that it is a completely new food donation network.

If I had to reduce the scope, I would keep the core flow—create a food listing, shortlist eligible recipients, record acceptance and track handover—and postpone advanced features such as automated distance calculation, notifications and complex analytics. A simple workflow that is easy to use is more valuable than adding features before validating the actual need.

AI assistance helped me organise the project structure, think through the matching workflow, improve documentation and prepare a clearer research comparison. It also helped me identify where the software must not make decisions, especially around food safety. However, I learned that AI-generated suggestions and synthetic sample data are not real-world evidence. I had to distinguish secondary research from primary field observations and avoid presenting assumptions, example records or reconstructed AI conversations as if I had personally collected them.

My next step is to visit one nearby food outlet or mess, ask permission to observe its process, and speak with a responsible person. I will record only what I actually see and what the person agrees to share. I will use that evidence to revise the problem statement, decide which features are genuinely useful and improve Mess2Meal based on a real user's needs.
